# TEAMFRAME — DISCOVERY PROPOSAL v1
## Exact copy · visual layout · three Page-1 variants · recommended PDF structure

**Built:** 17 August 2026 · **Purpose:** a working document for real prospect conversations, not a corporate brochure.
**Product basis:** TeamFrame exactly as it exists today. Every capability described below was verified against the canonical scope, the access model, the automation register and the leave service code. Nothing is aspirational.

**Constraints observed:** no pricing · no invented testimonials, ROI figures or compliance claims · no "FPORS", "readiness engine", "risk engine" or abstract people-ops language · no product-change recommendations · existing brand only.

## 1. Structure at a glance

Three assembled PDFs, three pages each. Pages 2 and 3 are identical across all three; only Page 1 changes.

| File | Page 1 | Page 2 | Page 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TeamFrame_Proposal_UAE.pdf` | **1A** — UAE professional / regulated | Common | Common |
| `TeamFrame_Proposal_Mauritius.pdf` | **1B** — Mauritius professional / regulated | Common | Common |
| `TeamFrame_Proposal_Startup.pdf` | **1C** — Startup / SaaS | Common | Common |

**Why three separate files rather than one document with an appendix:** a managing partner should receive a document that appears to have been written for their firm, not a generic one with their section flagged. It also makes the proposal an A/B test — after twenty conversations you will know which Page 1 produced the most recognition, and that is the fastest route to a settled positioning.

**Sequencing rule.** This is a *discovery* proposal, not a cold-outreach asset. Send it after a first conversation, or bring it to one. Sent cold it flattens the strongest asset available — a fifteen-year practitioner who has run this job — into a PDF.

## 2. Visual layout specification

### Format and grid
- **A4 portrait**, 210 × 297 mm.
- Margins: **19 mm** left/right, **17 mm** top, **15 mm** bottom.
- Single-column text measure capped at **150–152 mm** (≈ 78 characters) so body copy never runs the full width.
- Coverage grid on Page 2 is **three columns**, 6.5 mm gutter.

### Typography
| Role | Face | Size | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wordmark (masthead) | Charter, bold | 12.5 pt | Body ink `#2A2F37` |
| Segment label (masthead right) | Inter, semibold, uppercase, .15 em | 6.6 pt | Titanium Grey `#68707D` |
| Eyebrow | Inter, semibold, uppercase, .155 em | 7.1 pt | Titanium Grey |
| H1 (Page 1) | **Charter**, bold, −0.016 em | 25.5 pt / 1.12 | N-900 `#0F1115` |
| H2 (Pages 2–3) | Charter, bold | 14.6 pt | N-900 |
| Standfirst | Inter, regular | 9.4–10.1 pt / 1.56 | Body ink |
| Block heading | Inter, 650 weight | 9.2–10.3 pt | N-900 |
| Body | Inter, regular | 7.9–9.1 pt / 1.5 | Titanium Grey, with key phrases lifted to Body ink at 550 weight |
| Section label | Inter, semibold, uppercase, .155 em | 7.1 pt | Titanium Grey |

Serif for headlines and sans for everything else mirrors the type system already running in the product. Uppercase is used only for compact labels, per the brand system.

### Colour discipline
Electric Signal Green `#01FF22` appears in exactly **three** places per document, and nowhere else:
1. The **2.1 mm vertical stem** to the left of the Page 1 headline — the signature mark.
2. The **five numbered markers** on Page 2, as small filled squares with body-ink numerals.
3. The **single CTA button** on Page 3, body-ink text on lime.

Never lime body text. Never a large lime field. Never white text on lime. Never lime as a generic success colour. Everything else is the neutral scale — `#0F1115`, `#2A2F37`, `#68707D`, `#CCD1D8`, `#E9EDF1`, `#F4F6F8`, white — plus Soft Graphite `#42494D` for the one dark surface (the CTA panel), which matches the product's navigation rail and auth panel.

### Page architecture
- **Masthead** on every page: wordmark left, segment label right, hairline `#E9EDF1` rule beneath.
- **Footer** on every page: "Confidential — prepared for discussion" left, `n / 3` right, hairline rule above.
- **Page 1** is bottom-anchored: the four problem blocks sit under the standfirst, and the closing line plus the credential line are pinned to the foot of the page. The white space between is deliberate and is identical across all three variants.
- **Page 3** ends in a Soft Graphite panel containing the whole call to action, so the last thing on the page is the only thing to do.
- Colour is never the sole indicator anywhere: every numbered marker carries a numeral, every state carries a word.

## 3. Exact copy

### PAGE 1A — UAE professional and regulated services

> **Masthead right:** UAE — PROFESSIONAL & REGULATED FIRMS
> **Eyebrow:** FOR PROFESSIONAL AND REGULATED FIRMS IN THE UAE

**H1**
> The HR administration that nobody owns

**Standfirst**
> In most firms of twenty to seventy people there is no HR manager. The work still happens. It sits with an Operations Manager, an Administration Manager, a Finance Manager or a partner — alongside the job they were actually hired to do. Four things it usually looks like:

**01 — Documents and expiry dates live in a spreadsheet**
> Residence visas, Emirates IDs, passports and professional certifications all expire. Someone keeps a tracker. It is accurate until the week it isn't — and the cost of finding out late is a blocked renewal, a penalty, or an employee who cannot travel.

**02 — Policy sign-offs cannot be produced on request**
> Policies go out by email and people read them. Eighteen months later, when a regulator, an auditor or a client's due-diligence questionnaire asks **who acknowledged which version, and when**, the answer is a search through an inbox.

**03 — Whoever administers HR can see everything**
> The person who sets up the folders can see the salaries. In a partnership that is rarely what anyone intended, and it is rarely said out loud.

**04 — It depends on one person remembering**
> Probation dates, document requests, onboarding steps and exit checklists are carried in someone's head and their calendar. When they are away — or when they leave — it goes with them.

**Closing line**
> TeamFrame is an HR system built for firms in exactly this position. *It holds the employee record and runs the routine administration around it.*

**Credential line**
> Prepared by an HR practitioner with fifteen years running people operations across the UAE, Mauritius and international banking — including as the sole HR lead at a UAE company of the size this is built for.

**H1**

> It is not anyone's job, so it becomes everyone's job, and it gets done last. The cost is not usually a crisis — it is **inconsistency**, and answers that differ depending on who was asked.

> Two countries. Contractors alongside employees. A first manager layer. People asking questions that need the same answer every time — well before a full-time HR person is affordable or sensible.

### PAGE 2 — What TeamFrame does *(common to all three)*

**H2**
> One system for everyday HR administration

**Standfirst**
> TeamFrame holds the authoritative employee record and runs the routine administration around it. It is deliberately not a large HR platform — there is no recruitment pipeline, no performance ratings and no analytics suite to configure before it is useful.

**Section label:** WHAT IT COVERS

| | |
|---|---|
| **People** | Employees and contractors: employment type, position, department, location and reporting line. |
| **Onboarding** | Repeatable packs with owners and due dates, generated when someone is added. |
| **Leave** | Types, allocations, balances, requests, conflict checks, approval routing and absence history. |
| **Documents** | Requested from the employee, uploaded securely, stored privately and monitored for expiry. |
| **Policies** | Uploaded and versioned, published to the right people, acknowledged against the version. |
| **Probation & check-ins** | Reviews open at the right time; a short check-in reaches the employee at around thirty days. |
| **Employment changes** | Manager, position, department, location and contract changes, with an effective date and full history. |
| **Offboarding** | End date, handover, access removal and asset return as tracked steps; the position is released. |
| **Self-service** | Employees see their own profile, tasks, documents, policies and leave. Managers act for direct reports only. |

**Section label:** FIVE THINGS IT DOES THAT MOST SYSTEMS AT THIS SIZE DO NOT

**1 — A task cannot be closed without the evidence**
> "Upload the signed contract" stays open until a document is actually there. Marking it done is not an option. **What the system shows as complete is complete.**

**2 — Document expiry is monitored, not remembered**
> Every document with an expiry date is tracked. The renewal is raised before the date, the replacement satisfies the requirement automatically, and the previous version stays on file.

**3 — Acknowledgements are recorded against the policy version**
> Not "acknowledged the handbook" — **acknowledged version 3, on a date**. Superseded versions keep their history and stop generating obligations.

**4 — Salary and private files are separated from general HR access**
> Someone can administer people, leave, onboarding and documents **without seeing compensation or private employee files**. Payment and bank details sit separately again. Managers see their direct reports and no further.

**5 — The work appears without being scheduled**
> Adding an employee generates the onboarding. A start date sets the probation review. An expiry date raises the renewal. A departure generates the exit steps. You see decisions and exceptions, not a list of things to remember.

## 4. Copy decisions worth knowing

**The payroll statement is on Page 3, stated by the vendor, before the prospect asks.** Volunteering "we do not run payroll or generate WPS" converts the single most likely deal-breaker into a credibility signal. Withheld, it becomes something the buyer discovers later and reads as concealment.

**"What TeamFrame is not" is a full block, not a footnote.** In a category where the top measured buyer complaint is that total investment exceeded expectations, naming the boundaries early is a differentiator. It also disqualifies the wrong prospects before they cost you time.

**Every claim traces to something verified in the product.** Evidence-gated closure, expiry monitoring with automatic requirement satisfaction, version-specific acknowledgement with archived history, the People Operations / Salary / Private Documents / Payment access separation, direct-report-only manager scope, effective-dated employment changes, the ~30-day check-in, configurable working days and holidays, and the finance handoff. Nothing describes behaviour the product does not have.

**No number appears anywhere.** No pricing, no headcount claims, no time-saved figures, no percentages, no customer names. The only quantities are "twenty to seventy people", "thirty minutes" and "around thirty days", each of which is a description rather than a claim.

**The credential line is on Page 1, not buried.** It is currently in the footer of the live site. In a segment where the most recurring complaint across every vendor examined is that support collapses after the sale, a named practitioner who has done this job is the hardest thing in the category to copy — and it belongs where the buyer starts reading.

**Two words were deliberately avoided throughout:** "readiness" and "signal". Both are internal vocabulary. A managing partner does not translate them, and translation is friction at exactly the wrong moment.

## 5. Recommended final PDF structure

**Ship as three separate 3-page PDFs.** One per segment, matching the file table in §1.

| | Contents | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Page 1** | Segment-specific problem statement, four blocks, closing line, credential line | The only page that changes. This is the A/B test. |
| **Page 2** | What it covers (9 areas) + five differentiators + screenshot 1 | Common. The proof page. |
| **Page 3** | Four set-up facts + what it is not + screenshot 2 + CTA | Common. The disqualification-and-decision page. |

**Before the first send, three things must be done:**
1. Replace both screenshot placeholders with current captures to the briefs in §2, taken from the running application — not from the July 2026 archive.
2. Fill the contact block on Page 3 with a named person, an email and a direct number. The document should not leave without them.
3. Read Page 1 aloud in the voice of the specific firm you are sending it to. If a sentence would not survive being said to the managing partner's face, cut it.

**What deliberately is not in this version:** no pricing page, no case studies, no logo wall, no comparison table, no implementation timeline in weeks, no security appendix. Each of those is a real asset later. Each of them, added now, converts a discovery document into a brochure — and a brochure invites a procurement process instead of a conversation.

**A fourth page to add only when you have earned it:** a single page of what the first three customers actually said, in their words. Not testimonials you asked for — sentences you wrote down during discovery. That page cannot be written yet, and inventing it now would be the fastest way to lose the credibility the rest of the document is built on.
